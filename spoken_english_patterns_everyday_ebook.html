The central shift

A sentence can be grammatical and still feel translated. Natural conversation usually chooses a different package: a simpler verb, a stored chunk, a particle, a shorter status phrase, or a reaction that fits the situation.

Situation → English chunk → short spoken sentence

No milk → run out → We’ve run out of milk.
Door resists → won’t → The door won’t open.
Unexpected meeting → run into → I ran into Sarah.

The goal is not to make English childish. The logic can stay sophisticated. The surface gets lighter, faster, and more responsive.

Eight habits underneath the patterns
Describe the eventNo milk → we ran out. Cold now → it got cold. Failed opening → it won’t open.
Prefer verbsWe had a discussion → we talked. I gave an explanation → I explained.
Retrieve chunksHead home. Come over. Pick me up. Sort it out. Hang on a sec.
Use particlesUp, down, out, back, off, away. Tiny words carry huge amounts of everyday meaning.
Let context workNot sure yet. Ready? Want to come? Either works. You do not need to say everything.
Report status compactlyI’m done. I’m back. She’s out. I’m off. We’re behind. I’m running late.
React before explainingYeah, fair. The thing is… / Maybe. I’m just not sure… / Sounds good.
Keep the rhythmShort reaction → slightly longer reason → short conclusion.
